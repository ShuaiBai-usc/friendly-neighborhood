FRIENDLY NEIGHBORHOOD // MECHANISM← BACK TO SITE

Water pressure holds it open.
Lose the loop, the spring shuts it.

A thin, fragile pipe loop wraps the house and stays full of pressurized water. That pressure holds a spring-loaded gate up, out of the service line. When fire melts the loop or the house collapses onto it, the pressure is gone. The spring drives the gate down and water to the house stops. No power, no electronics in the shutoff path.

Four moves

01

Armed

The loop is a dead end fed from the service line, so it sits at line pressure. That pressure on the piston beats the spring and holds the gate up.

02

Loop breaks

Fire softens and bursts the thin loop, or the collapsing house crushes it. A small trickle escapes, faster than the feed orifice can refill, so pressure falls.

03

Spring wins

Chamber pressure falls below the spring's trip point. The stored spring drives the piston and blade down across the service line.

04

Reset handle

Cap or repair the loop, then pull the reset handle. It lifts the piston off the feed port so pressure can rebuild and re-cock the spring. Let go: water pressure now holds the gate open.

Hosing the house does not trip it: the loop has no outlet, so hose flow never drains it. Only a breach in the loop does.
